The long game

What Transition Planning Covers

Transition planning means preparing your child for what's next — whether that's moving from preschool to elementary, elementary to middle school, or building the life skills that lead to real-world independence. We don't wait until the transition is six weeks away. We build toward it from day one.

  • School readiness and school-to-school transitions
  • Community participation — stores, restaurants, public spaces
  • Safety awareness and self-advocacy skills
  • Pre-vocational skills for older children
  • Gradual reduction of ABA support as skills are mastered
School readiness

Preparing for School Transitions

Changing schools is hard for any child. For a child with autism, it can feel seismic. We make sure your child walks in ready — not overwhelmed.

IEP & 504 collaboration

Your BCBA works alongside your child's school team to align goals, share progress data, and recommend accommodations that actually work.

Classroom-ready behaviors

Sitting, attending, following group instructions, raising a hand — the skills your child needs before the first bell rings.

Peer interaction skills

Taking turns, sharing materials, joining group play. We teach the social building blocks before your child needs them in a new classroom.

Routine practice & anxiety support

We rehearse the new school day at home — earlier wake-ups, different lunch times, new hallways — so the unfamiliar becomes familiar before day one.

Beyond the classroom

Building Independence at Home and in the Community

Daily living skills

Chores, hygiene, meal prep — age-appropriate independence that makes every day a little easier for your child and your family. Each mastered skill is one less thing that requires adult support.

Safety skills

Crossing streets, recognizing danger, handling emergencies. These aren't abstract lessons — they're practiced in real environments until your child can respond reliably when it matters.

Community participation

Structured outings to stores, restaurants, and public spaces — with support that gradually fades as your child builds confidence. The goal is access, not avoidance.

Self-management

Self-monitoring, self-evaluation, and self-correction. We teach your child to manage their own behavior — so they don't need someone else doing it for them.

Planning for what's next

When ABA Services End: Planning for Success

ABA shouldn't last forever — and that's a sign of success, not failure. We plan for discharge from the very beginning.

Gradual service reduction

As your child masters goals, we reduce hours and support step by step. No abrupt stops — a controlled fade that proves skills are holding without us.

Training parents & school staff

We teach you and your child's school team how to maintain gains independently. The strategies shouldn't disappear when the therapist does.

Follow-up support

Discharge doesn't mean abandonment. We offer follow-up check-ins and consultation so your family has a safety net as your child moves forward.
