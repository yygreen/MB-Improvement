# Build record: `/post/how-much-is-autism-benefits` (2026-08-13, published)

**Item:** `66b0609d5dbbc7dac092008a` · Blog Posts `6627fd62e242d50407cfe157`

The highest-value page in the financing cluster and the one the cluster
audits kept missing. GSC over the last 30 days: **302 clicks, 42,175
impressions, 0.72% CTR, average position 7.5**. It ranks; it does not get
clicked; and until today it did not link to a single one of the three state
guides we spent the phase building.

It stayed invisible through three rounds of auditing because the ad-hoc slug
regex in use at the time matched `insurance|cost|financ|medicaid|...` and
never included `benefits`. That method failure is fixed separately in
`tools/seo/sweep-cluster.py`.

## Pass 1: figures and metadata (+234 chars)

Six replacements, each asserted to match exactly once, PATCHed over REST,
re-read and diffed. Verified byte-identical.

The SSI figures were two years stale and hedged rather than corrected. The
page said "As of 2024 ... $943 ... $1,415 ... $3,822 ... current-year
figures are slightly higher", which is a page telling a family to go and
find the real number themselves.

Replaced with the 2026 federal benefit rate, verified at ssa.gov:

> The monthly maximum Federal amounts for 2026 are $994 for an eligible
> individual, $1,491 for an eligible individual with an eligible spouse,
> and $498 for an essential person.

- table header `2024 Federal Maximum` to `2026 Federal Maximum`
- `$943 / month` to `$994 / month`
- `$3,822 / month` to "Set by the parent's earnings record". That figure was
  the SSDI maximum presented as though it were an autism benefit ceiling.
  SSDI has no single publishable ceiling, so the honest answer is the rule,
  not a number.
- an unsourced `$1,500` average removed
- `/post/average-cost-of-autism-treatment` repointed to
  `/post/cost-of-aba-therapy-for-autism`. That was a link into one of the
  three pages queued for a 301, so the page was pointing at a URL we intend
  to retire.

Metadata, aimed at the 42k impressions rather than the ranking:

    name:         How Much Are Autism Benefits? SSI, SSDI and Medicaid
    post-summary: SSI pays up to $994 a month in 2026. What autism benefits
                  actually pay, who qualifies, and why most families receive
                  less than the federal maximum.

The old title read "How Much is Autism Benefits", which is broken grammar in
a title tag on a page with 42,000 impressions. The new meta leads with a
concrete figure, which is the lever available on a 0.72% CTR at position 7.5.

## Pass 2: the state section (+1,093 chars)

`<h2>State-Mandated Autism Benefits</h2>` profiled **California, Arizona and
Florida** in three tables, and gave the three states we actually serve one
paragraph with no sources and no links. Every row in those tables said
"Required" or "Included" with nothing behind it.

Replaced with New Jersey, Georgia and North Carolina, generated by
`tools/blog/build-benefits-state-section.py` from `data/funding/*.json`, so
every figure traces to the statute or the insurance regulator.

Each state gets its statute, the ages the mandate reaches, the dollar cap
with its catch attached, the visit-limit position, and a link to its guide.

The caps are the reason this had to be generated rather than written:

| State | Cap | Why it cannot be printed bare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New Jersey | $36,000 | `rarely_applicable`. DOBI has said it often cannot lawfully be applied. |
| Georgia | $35,000 | `applies_to_aba_only`. A ceiling a plan may apply, not a budget. |
| North Carolina | $40,000 | `stale_base_indexed`. A 2015 base the statute indexes; the live figure is not published anywhere citable. |

The build gate enforces exactly that: it finds each cap literal in the copy
and reads the 200 characters that follow, failing the build if the sentence
stops at the figure. A stale base printed without its catch is worse than no
figure at all, because a family reads it as their ceiling.

Also fixed here: **"Every state in the U.S. now has some form of autism
insurance mandate"**, the same sweeping unsourced claim still open on
`/financial-aid-resources`. Hedged to "most states", with the weight moved
onto the three states we can source.

Added a closing paragraph on the two things that actually decide whether any
mandate reaches a family: whether the employer self-funds, and the state
Medicaid position. Neither was mentioned anywhere in the section before.
