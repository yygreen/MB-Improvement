# Hero image prompt, aba-therapy-funding-new-jersey

Replaces the typographic card currently on the draft
(asset `6a7b37583152a720e4ee8d14`). Written to the same house spec as the
twelve state service pages, with the differences noted at the bottom.

## Spec

    aspect        16/9 (the current hero is 1400x788)
    delivery      WebP, >= 1600x900
    composition   subject left of centre; the blog template puts the title
                  beneath, so the right third can carry light and negative space
    safe area     outer 6% expendable
    thumbnail     also crop a 600x338 version; it must still read at 300px wide

## The prompt

> Candid documentary lifestyle photograph. Real, lived-in American family home,
> not a set. Natural window light only. Warm neutral palette. 35mm, eye level,
> f/2.0, shallow depth of field. Unposed and mid-moment: nobody looks at the
> camera. Photographic realism, no illustration, no HDR.
>
> A mother in her thirties sits at a kitchen table in early evening, one hand
> resting on an open laptop she is not looking at, the other holding a single
> sheet of correspondence she has clearly read more than once. A phone lies face
> up beside a mug. Two or three more sheets and a torn envelope sit in a loose
> pile at her elbow. Her expression is concentrated rather than distressed:
> someone working a problem, not someone defeated by one. A child's cereal bowl
> and a small wooden toy sit at the far end of the table, out of focus, the only
> sign that this is about a child. The child is not in frame.
>
> New Jersey house: hardwood floor, plaster walls, wide painted trim, a
> cast-iron radiator under a double-hung window, close neighbouring rooflines and
> mature maples visible outside, cool late-afternoon four-season light.
>
> Exclude: clinic, therapy office, classroom, waiting room. No medical equipment,
> clipboards, charts, scrubs, lanyards or ID badges. No logos, signage, screens
> showing content, or any legible text. No puzzle-piece imagery or
> autism-awareness symbolism. No posed stock smiles, no eye contact with the
> camera, no thumbs-up. No watermarks. No insurance-company branding of any kind.

## The three exclusions that are not stylistic

- **No clinic.** Program-wide copy gate, and this page sells in-home
  specifically. An institutional room contradicts the page it sits on.
- **No puzzle piece.** Widely rejected by autistic people and much of the
  advocacy world. On a BCBA-owned provider it would do real damage.
- **No legible text on the paperwork.** This matters more here than on the
  service pages. A hero for a funding guide will naturally want to show a denial
  letter, and a generated one will invent a carrier name, a policy number and a
  dollar figure. That is a fabricated document on a page whose entire argument is
  that every number is sourced. Keep every sheet out of focus and unreadable, and
  reject any candidate where a word can be made out.

## Why this scene rather than a therapy scene

The page is not about therapy. It is about the hour a parent spends at a table
working out who is supposed to pay, which is the moment the reader is actually
in when they land here. A photo of a session would be a nicer picture and a
worse match.

The current typographic card is a reasonable fallback and stays on the draft
until a photograph is chosen. It says the same thing with less warmth.

## What to check before accepting a candidate

1. Zoom to 200% on every sheet of paper. If any word resolves, reject.
2. Confirm no lanyard, badge, scrub top or clipboard crept in.
3. Confirm the room could not be mistaken for a waiting area.
4. Check the 600x338 thumbnail crop still reads as a person at a table, not as
   an abstract of hands and paper.
5. Confirm her expression does not read as crying or despairing. The page is
   about leverage a family has, not about how hard this is.
