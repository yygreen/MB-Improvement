## EMF: the draft's grade was wrong

The draft called this "the strongest of the five... REORGANISE, not rewrite",
on the basis that the live page already concluded research "has not provided
consistent support". Reading the whole body showed that conclusion sat inside a
page that also:

- presented a Spanish case-control study as showing a correlation between
  paternal job-related EMF exposure and ASD, in a table headed "Key Findings"
- cited Richard Lathe's microwave hypothesis as if it were evidence, alongside
  the observation that autism diagnoses rose as microwaves spread
- asserted children are "particularly vulnerable to EMF radiation" because of
  "thinner skulls and developing brains"
- ran a "Recommendations and Precautions" section advising families to limit
  wireless devices, buy low-EMF appliances, "Use EMF Shields", and "Monitor EMF
  Levels" with EMF meters

That last part is the reason this was not a reorganise. The page was giving
families a shopping list. All of it is gone.

Also dropped, from the DRAFT rather than the live page: its claims about the
National Toxicology Program and US Navy/Air Force radiofrequency cohorts. They
concern cancer rather than autism and could not be verified here, and asserting
unverified specifics is the exact failure being corrected. Replaced with an
accurate characterization of the literature: a PubMed sweep of EMF terms against
autism returns ~178 records, overwhelmingly studies that USE phones and
wearables as research instruments (smartphone screening, EEG, remote
assessment), plus a small number of mechanism papers. No epidemiology comparing
exposed and unexposed children. The article now says that plainly and notes
the absence is itself informative.

The live page did warn about chelation, but laid the protocol out in neutral
feature tables ("Detox Method | Description": chelation, sauna, herbal), which
reads as a menu. Replaced with the evidence position up front.

Cochrane PMID resolved: 26114777 is the current pub2 record; 26106752 is the
superseded original. The draft was right and the earlier session note was
wrong. Quoted verbatim: "no clinical trial evidence was found to suggest that
pharmaceutical chelation is an effective intervention for ASD", plus the
risks-outweigh-benefits conclusion citing hypocalcaemia, renal impairment and
reported death.

Dropped from the draft: the specific death of a five-year-old during a chelation
session in Pennsylvania in 2005. It is widely reported, but it could not be
sourced from here, and publishing an unsourced account of a named child's death
is not something to do on a client site. The Cochrane review's own "reported
death" carries the same point with a citation behind it. Worth revisiting if a
citable source is available.

Also: "our clinical team" -> "our team" (no clinic implied), and the live page's
732.507.9883 is gone.
