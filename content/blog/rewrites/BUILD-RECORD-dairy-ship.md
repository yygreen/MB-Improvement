# Ship record: dairy-and-autism (2026-08-11)

Fifth of the seven. Live item 66bda846056fb4ca7d2ba438, slug kept.

## The draft's verdict understated the problem

The draft graded this page "REORGANISE, not rewrite... the mildest of the five
and hedged throughout". Reading the live body showed otherwise. It carried:

- a causal claim for casein: antibodies to casein "could potentially contribute
  to the development of autism"
- a second one for beta-casomorphin-7, which "could have a negative effect on
  the brain and contribute to the symptoms of autism"
- the opioid theory presented as physiology: withdrawing dairy may produce
  "withdrawal symptoms that resemble those associated with opiates"
- probiotics described as "observed to improve both behavioral and
  gastrointestinal symptoms... by restoring gut microbiota equilibrium"
- a promotional aside for camel milk, linking to the article we rewrote
  yesterday to say the pooled trials found no significant effect. The two pages
  contradicted each other.
- "A study published in the Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders",
  twice, with no author or year
- a reference list of bare PMC URLs numbered 1, 3, 5, 6
- 732.507.9883 again, alongside 732.813.7333 on the same page

So it was replaced wholesale rather than reorganised.

## Sourcing added

The draft argued well but cited almost nothing. Now:
- McElhanon et al. 2014, Pediatrics (24777214): GI symptoms OR 4.42
  (95% CI 1.90-10.28); constipation 3.86, diarrhea 3.63, abdominal pain 2.45.
- Li et al. 2021, Eur J Pediatr (33145704): association between food
  hypersensitivity and ASD. Cited for the association only; the paper's
  speculation about ASD onset is NOT repeated as our claim.
- Keller 2021 (33573238) and Piwowarczyk 2018 (28612113) for the casein claim.
  Piwowarczyk kept deliberately: it was the live page's own citation, and its
  conclusion ("little evidence that a GFCF diet is beneficial") supports the
  rewrite rather than the text it used to sit under.

Both prevalence findings are explicitly framed as associations that "do not
tell us which way the arrow runs", consistent with the lesson the vitamin D and
GFCF pages now teach.

## New rule applied

First article shipped under the null-assertion rule from the GFCF correction.
Casein now reads "the trials have not been able to show that it works" and "not
the same as proving there is none". The gate fails the build on "does not
work", "has no effect", or "proves it does not" appearing in our own voice.

## Scope deliberately narrowed

Dropped from the old page: milk-consumption comparisons, sugar-sweetened
beverage associations, and the general feeding-problems section. They are real
topics but they are not "dairy and autism", and the draft's whole point was to
stop this page blurring a medical question into a discredited one. Feeding
selectivity is a good candidate for its own page.

No chart. The three-questions structure is the argument here, and it reads as
prose.
